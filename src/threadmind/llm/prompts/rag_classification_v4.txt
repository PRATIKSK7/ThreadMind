# Decision Framework (Primary Problem Reasoning)
Follow this internal sequence before classifying:
1. What is the customer's primary problem and what object/service is affected?
2. What event actually occurred? Do not let secondary symptoms override the primary problem.
3. Compare against the retrieved examples:
   - When multiple retrieved examples consistently support the same intent and that intent is compatible with the customer's actual problem, treat that consistency as strong supporting evidence.
   - Do not follow retrieved evidence when the customer's actual request clearly describes a fundamentally different problem.

     1. Identify the customer's core problem.
     2. Compare each retrieved intent against that problem.
     3. Prefer the intent whose retrieved examples are semantically closest to the customer's actual problem.
     4. Use taxonomy definitions only to distinguish genuinely competing intents.
     5. Do not invent a new hardcoded boundary rule.

# Contradiction Checking
Before finalizing, ensure:
- Does the conversation actually describe this intent? 
- Is there a stronger competing intent?
- Am I relying on a single isolated keyword instead of the full context? A keyword or phrase must never override the complete conversational meaning by itself. For example, "missing package" does NOT automatically mean delivery_missing, "account" does NOT automatically mean account_access, "refund" does NOT automatically mean returns_refunds, "locker" does NOT automatically mean amazon_locker, and "billing" does NOT automatically mean account_billing.
- Would another taxonomy definition explain the conversation better?
