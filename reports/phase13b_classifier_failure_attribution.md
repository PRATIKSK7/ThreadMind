# Phase 13B — Classifier Failure Attribution

## Executive Summary
Out of 196 benchmark queries, the V2 classifier failed on 24 (12.24%).
Crucially, in **19 out of 24 failures (79.2%)**, the correct intent *was* present in the top-3 retrieved candidates. The classifier either ignored it or was confused by competing retrieved intents.

## Taxonomy Ambiguity
Some failures (especially `promotions_pricing` vs `other_support` or `account_access` vs `delivery_wrong_item`) exhibit genuine taxonomic overlap in the conversation text, leading the LLM to choose the broader `other_support` label.

## Corpus Label Noise
The retrieval corpus was generated by a rule-based baseline. In cases where the retrieved intents contain conflicting evidence, it is highly likely the retrieved examples themselves were mislabelled by the baseline, injecting contradictory signals into the prompt context.

## V2 Prompt Weaknesses
1. **No strategy for conflicting evidence**: When RAG retrieves examples from 2 or 3 different intents, the V2 prompt does not tell the LLM how to resolve the tie (e.g., trust the first example, or trust the majority).
2. **Over-correction against retrieval**: The prompt explicitly warns: `DO NOT classify based solely on the closest retrieved example`. This causes the LLM to second-guess unanimous, perfect retrieval (causing the 8 CLASSIFIER_IGNORED_CORRECT failures).
3. **Weak boundaries**: The prompt lacks disambiguation rules for edge cases like `promotions_pricing` vs `account_billing`.

## Root Cause
**MIXED**

The bottleneck is split. Exactly 33.3% of failures are `RETRIEVAL_MISSING` (a hard retrieval ceiling). However, 66.7% of failures occur *despite* successful retrieval, pointing directly at `CLASSIFIER_REASONING` and the prompt's inability to resolve conflicting RAG context or its tendency to over-correct.
